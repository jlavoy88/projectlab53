^Little Talk About Honesty, 2021.

The renowned Cuban artist Esterio Segura (b. 1970) is part of the '90s Generation', so named on the basis of the shared characteristics of the period in which this generation grew up and came of age. Segura's work is distinguished by its versatility. It moves among the numerous artistic expressions of drawing, painting, sculpture, photography, and installation. His creative combinations of varying materials and resources, common-use objects, and artifacts contribute to his work in both conceptual and morphological terms, as his aesthetic solutions break the boundaries of pre-established formulas and incorporate premises from trends such as pop art and arte povera. Consequently, Segura has developed a narrative abundant in the connections as well as the contradictions of history, religion, identity, migration, and everyday life – a narrative reflecting both complexities and singularities. In Segura's art, the irony, subtle humor, and parables nuance the stories he tells, contributing to a polysemy that expands and diversifies the dialogue he engages with his work.

^The Butterfly Charmer, 2021.

The present exhibition, titled "In the Distance," is centered on the Italian fictional figure of Pinocchio. This cultural icon represents the eternal struggle between truth and lie, between good and evil. In the series "History Bites its Tail" ("La Historia se muerde la cola"), Segura deploys Pinocchio to catalyze the rethinking of the premises of historical evolution from a perspective made more questioning by the adoption of the tone of sincerity in childhood inquiry. The spirit of the project is not to propose answers, but to formulate questions about national and international reality and the fluid dynamics of our recorded history. Segura's Pinocchios renounce the uselessness of solutions that are mere rhetoric and propose as the only form of truth the naivete of children's questions, stripped of all prejudice.

^Under His Own Shadow, 2021.

Although the artist is recognized for his three-dimensional work including sculptures and installations of the figure of Pinocchio, his contemporary approach to this figure/symbol is from a two-dimensional perspective – drawings, watercolors, and mixed media. "In the Distance" focuses specifically on the two-dimensional work in which this global character embodying the message of truth versus lies leads us down a path of questions. The title of the show takes its name from one of the pieces included, in which a hangman's noose is connected to the end of Pinocchio's very long wooden nose – suggesting a distant future reality for the character.

^To Build the Guilt and Share it, 2022.

The sixteen mixed media artworks, most never before exhibited, present to the visitor an array of creative expression and experience. Three of the works – A Story Named Pearl Harbor, The Medieval Knights, Emperor – invite the viewer to travel through time. Four of them – Choreography, French Perfume, Grandma, Gases Go to Heaven like My Soul? – explore the concepts of geography. And yet others – Waiting for Shakespeare at the Agreed Time, Welcome Home, What Happened Half an Hour After Lola Was Killed? – invite the viewer to consider enigmatic scenes and questions.

^Welcome Home, 2022. Gladiator, 2022.

Throughout the body of work, the iconography and style of the representation of Pinocchio varies, ranging from influences of the Japanese and Russian illustrations to more classical and childish characteristics.

^In the Distance, 2021.

"In the Distance", on display during the month of May at the Sager Reeves Gallery, is a passage to the artist's storytelling and continuing queries.
